Join the Code Writers Guild Reading Group — a monthly forum for anyone at Dartmouth who writes code. Whether you're a software engineer, research software engineer, data scientist, or researcher who codes, this group is for you.

Each month we pick a short piece of content — a book chapter, conference talk, or blog post — and discuss how the ideas apply to the work we do at Dartmouth. Sessions are informal, low-pressure, and designed for conversation, not quizzes. No preparation is required to attend.

This Month's Topic: "Programming by Coincidence" from The Pragmatic Programmer

Have you ever had code that worked… and you weren't quite sure why? In this short, punchy chapter, Andrew Hunt and David Thomas argue that developers too often rely on luck — code that happens to work — rather than deliberately understanding why it works. They draw a sharp line between "accidents of implementation" and intentional design, and make a compelling case for coding deliberately: understanding your assumptions, relying only on documented behavior, and testing both your beliefs and your code.

We'll use this chapter as a jumping-off point to discuss our own experiences with coincidental code, the dangers of relying on undocumented behavior, and what it actually means to code deliberately in the kind of work we do at Dartmouth.
