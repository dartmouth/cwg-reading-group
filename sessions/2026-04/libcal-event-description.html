Join us for the first meeting of the Code Writers Guild Reading Group — a new monthly forum for anyone at Dartmouth who writes code. Whether you're a software engineer, research software engineer, data scientist, or researcher who codes, this group is for you.

Each month we pick a short piece of content — a conference talk, blog post, or article — and discuss how the ideas apply to the work we do at Dartmouth. Sessions are informal, low-pressure, and designed for conversation, not quizzes. No preparation is required to attend.

This Month's Topic: "All the Little Things" by Sandi Metz

In this widely loved conference talk (~38 min), Sandi Metz takes a notoriously tangled chunk of code and refactors it live on stage, turning a messy 43-line conditional into a set of small, clear objects. Along the way she makes a provocative argument: duplication is far cheaper than the wrong abstraction.

We'll use the talk as a jumping-off point to discuss refactoring courage, when (and when not) to abstract, and how these ideas play out in the kind of code we write at Dartmouth.
