Join the Code Writers Guild Reading Group, a monthly forum for anyone at Dartmouth who writes code. Whether you're a software engineer, research software engineer, data scientist, or researcher who codes, this group is for you.

Each month we pick a short piece of content, like a book chapter, conference talk, or blog post, and discuss how the ideas apply to the work we do at Dartmouth. Sessions are informal, low-pressure, and aimed at sparking conversation. No preparation is required to attend.

This Month's Topic: "AI Made Me Doubt Everything About Programming" by Felienne Hermans

Felienne Hermans teaches computer science at a university and at a high school in Amsterdam, and created Hedy, a programming language for teaching. In this keynote from DDD Europe 2026, she explains why she fell out of love with computer science. She was told spreadsheets aren't "real programming." She found that most mainstream languages reject Arabic numerals as invalid characters. And she noticed that our field rewards work for being hard rather than for being useful.

Using examples from glaciology to chess, she argues that the AI hype is the latest sign of a field that thinks it is neutral when it isn't. She asks what programming is actually for. Her answer is that the future isn't inevitable: we choose how we build, and we can choose differently.

We'll use the talk to discuss who counts as a "real" programmer, what kinds of work get valued in our jobs, where we would (or wouldn't) let AI into our craft, and who our code is really for.
