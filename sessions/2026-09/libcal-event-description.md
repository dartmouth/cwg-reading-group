## Full Description

Join the Code Writers Guild Reading Group, a monthly forum for anyone at Dartmouth who writes code. Whether you're a software engineer, research software engineer, data scientist, or researcher who codes, this group is for you.

Each month we pick a short piece of content, like a book chapter, conference talk, or blog post, and discuss how the ideas apply to the work we do at Dartmouth. Sessions are informal, low-pressure, and aimed at sparking conversation. No preparation is required to attend.

**This Month's Topic: "A Developer By Any Other Name or 'Catch That Imposter!'" and how we deal with imposter syndrome in code writing and development.

Continuing the thread from our last discussion, a thought-provoking (terrifying?) question bubbles up: What/who is a code writer? What about one that doesn’t write code? Using whichever metaphor comes to mind and offers some means of explicating your perspective, you may be the “designer” in desktop publishing or the “architect” of a set of specs, tools, and environments—technical and social—that enable your team to build the thing. Some might argue that in order for you to do this, you need to be able to play all positions on the team with great skill. For others, you may be served with the understanding that all it takes are the right intentions.

But there is a third opinion, and one that is most insidious: you may actually be an imposter and not know enough to be considered a real code writer. 

What does it mean to be able to write code? Who or what determines that skillset and skill level? Do accomplishments or completed projects indicate skill, or do tough coding exercises and having theoretical discussions show more prowess? Are our personal standards for what it means to be a code writer too high? And if not, how do we actually achieve them? Is this all just a zero-sum game!?! Join us for an engaging discussion on imposter syndrome and what it really means to be a code writer. 


**Read before attending (optional but encouraged):** "What we talk about when we talk about imposter syndrome", by Eira May, available online: [Access via the Stack Overflow blog](https://stackoverflow.blog/2023/09/11/what-we-talk-about-when-we-talk-about-imposter-syndrome/?utm_campaign=the-overflow-newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_source=iterable)
